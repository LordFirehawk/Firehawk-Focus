# Ambient audio in Firehawk Focus

Researched 26 October 2026. Research only; no application changes or sound downloads. Asset metadata and license labels were checked against publisher pages. No recordings were auditioned, processed, or tested for seamless playback, and no runtime prototype was built.

## Recommendation

Yes, we can build the sound-mixing and Pomodoro functionality described. Firehawk already has the timer. Adding independently adjustable ambient layers, saved mixes, and playback that can follow the timer is technically straightforward with native audio APIs. Producing a library that stays pleasant over long sessions requires more care than adding the sliders.

Use individually verified CC0 recordings for the initial library. CC0 expressly allows commercial copying, modification, and redistribution, which suits both bundled sounds and this project's public source distribution. Attribution is optional, but retain credits and source records. CC0 does not clear third-party music or privacy rights contained in a recording, and the uploader's license label is not a warranty. [Creative Commons CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/)

My recommendation is an optional Sounds panel inside Firehawk, with an independent playback mode. This gives us timer integration and a useful mixer when no focus session is running, without requiring another application.

## What Noisli currently offers

Noisli's official feature page describes 28 sounds, individual volume controls, combinations, saved favorites, curated playlists, gradual volume oscillation, and shuffling between mixes. Its timer supports sessions and breaks, optional automatic starts, and stopping or fading the sound when time expires. These are all practical features for us to implement. [Noisli features](https://www.noisli.com/features)

The supplied [playlists URL](https://www.noisli.com/playlists) redirected to login during research, so I used the public feature documentation. I did not inspect or listen to its authenticated player. I have not verified the claim that Noisli has added no sounds for five to seven years. This recommendation does not depend on that history. We would source our own recordings and build our own interface.

## Which free libraries fit redistribution?

The permission we need is to include independently playable recordings in an app, potentially with raw audio in a public repository. Permission to use a sound under a video or in a game does not automatically establish that permission.

| Source | Assessment for this project |
| --- | --- |
| Freesound, individually verified CC0 | Preferred. We can edit, bundle, and redistribute the selected recording under its CC0 terms. Freesound also hosts other licenses, so selection must happen per file. |
| OpenGameArt, individually verified CC0 | Another suitable source. Check the submission and any attached notices rather than assuming the whole site uses one license. |
| Public-domain recordings | Useful where the original source and dedication are clear. Prefer provenance we can document. |
| CC BY recordings | Another possible route if we provide the required attribution, license link, and change notices. Exact obligations depend on the version. [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) |
| Pixabay Content License | Not my recommendation for the bundled mixer. Its standalone restriction creates uncertainty when recordings remain independently playable. Adding sliders or transcoding does not establish a new creative work. [License summary](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/), [binding terms, sections 4–5](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/) |
| Mixkit | Poor fit for public audio assets. Its sound-effect license excludes redistribution on its own, in a tool or template, or with source files. Obtain specific clearance if considering it. [Sound Effects Free License](https://mixkit.co/license/modal/sfxFree/) |
| Sonniss GDC bundles | Free commercial production use is allowed, but supplying the sound effects themselves is restricted, including certain device or service outputs. An ambience mixer needs clarification; public sound-file redistribution needs permission. [GDC license](https://sonniss.com/gdc-bundle-license/) |

The last three assessments are interpretations of the published restrictions, not claims that every focus app is explicitly prohibited. There is little reason to accept that ambiguity when CC0 alternatives exist. Avoid noncommercial-only recordings if future commercial distribution is possible. Do not treat the repository's MIT license as the license for third-party sounds.

## How to make sounds loop and combine well

Ambient layers do not need to share tempo or duration. Rain can loop every 73 seconds while a water recording loops every 139 seconds. Musical layers need more attention to harmony and rhythm, so I would begin with environmental audio and generated noise.

There are two separate problems: an audible seam in the recording, and a playback gap introduced by the player or codec. Both need checking. A loop flag does not repair a mismatch between the first and last samples.

Proposed preparation workflow, not work performed during this research:

1. Audition original files on headphones for clipping, handling noise, wind rumble, speech, music, and distracting events. Reject unsuitable recordings rather than trying to repair everything.
2. Select stable sections. Prefer roughly one to three minutes when worthwhile, but judge repetition by ear rather than duration alone.
3. Prepare the loop with matching boundaries or an overlap crossfade. Do not simply fade every recording to silence, which makes the repeat obvious. Audacity supports crossfading clips and smoothing waveform discontinuities. [Audacity crossfade documentation](https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/crossfade_clips.html)
4. Match perceived loudness across the library, then reserve mixing headroom. Six independently loud layers can overload the combined output. Test maximum combinations and use conservative gains or output limiting where appropriate.
5. Keep lossless masters. Choose delivery encoding after checking decoder support and loop behavior on both platforms. PCM WAV is a useful baseline; compressed files reduce distribution size but still require boundary testing.
6. Audition repeated boundaries and combinations over a full focus session. Keep license evidence, creator, source URL, acquisition date, file hash, and an editing record with each chosen asset.

Independent gain controls make combining sounds easy. Smooth volume changes prevent clicks; perceptual slider scaling makes quieter settings easier to select. Qt's own documentation recommends nonlinear scaling for volume controls. [Qt AudioOutput](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qml-qtmultimedia-audiooutput.html)

| Distribution | [Package.swift](../../platforms/macos/Package.swift) copies a Sounds resource directory. | Bundle a small offline starter pack. Keep a catalog of IDs, labels, files, credits, and loop information so new sounds do not require new playback logic. |

Apple documents simultaneous playback with multiple `AVAudioPlayer` instances, looping, individual volume, and fades. These capabilities are sufficient for a small native mixer. Use `AVAudioEngine` if procedural generation, a shared processing graph, or more advanced mixing becomes necessary. [AVAudioPlayer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudioplayer?changes=_3), [looping](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudioplayer/numberofloops?changes=l_8_9), [fades](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudioplayer/setvolume(_:fadeduration:)), [Apple audio overview](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/audio-and-music?changes=_5&language=objc)

On Linux, Qt provides a `MediaPlayer` with infinite looping and a corresponding `AudioOutput` for gain control. This is a plausible starting point for long ambience. `SoundEffect` favors low-latency WAV effects; its documentation suggests MediaPlayer when low latency is unnecessary because it supports more formats and uses fewer resources. Documentation establishes available controls, not gapless performance in our host. [Qt MediaPlayer](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qml-qtmultimedia-mediaplayer.html), [Qt SoundEffect](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qml-qtmultimedia-soundeffect.html)

A separate web mixer is also feasible. Web Audio specifies looped audio buffers, per-layer gain, mixing, and audio-time scheduling. It would introduce another UI and browser lifecycle constraints, so it is a secondary option rather than my first recommendation for this native app. [W3C Web Audio specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/)

## Proposed first version

Start with six to eight carefully prepared sounds, per-sound toggles and sliders, master volume and mute, and named saved mixes. Candidate categories are rain, ocean, stream, forest, fireplace, café, and generated noise. The final selection should follow auditioning.

Offer two playback modes: follow the focus timer, or play independently. In timer mode, define pause, break, completion, skip, and reset behavior explicitly. Fade or reduce ambience before the completion alert. Make a quiet transition into breaks the default, with continuous playback available as a preference.

For easy access, put the current mix and play/pause control on the timer screen, with one action opening all sound controls. Presets should restore both enabled sounds and their volumes. Examples for auditioning are rain with low fireplace, stream with sparse birds, and café with low rain. These combinations are suggestions, not mixes tested here.

Leave playlist shuffle and gradual volume oscillation for a later pass. Importing a user's own files would be a valuable later addition because it lets the library grow beyond shipped recordings.

No sound API subscription, streaming backend, or account system is required for bundled local playback. Adding new recordings would primarily mean asset preparation, credits, and catalog entries.

## Size and remaining uncertainty

Illustrative calculations, not measurements of a build:

- One minute of stereo 48 kHz, 16-bit PCM takes about 11.52 MB before container overhead. Eight one-minute tracks take about 92.16 MB.
- At an assumed 160–192 kbps, eight one-minute encoded tracks total about 9.6–11.52 MB. Their quality and looping must still be checked.
- If six one-minute stereo tracks are fully decoded to 48 kHz, 32-bit float buffers, their samples alone occupy about 138.24 MB. Use an approach appropriate to the chosen player and asset lengths rather than loading the entire catalog into memory.

A small mixer is a bounded addition, but a reliable estimate should follow a three-layer playback experiment. The unresolved checks are audible source quality, actual loop seams after delivery encoding, clipping under combinations, CPU and memory use, and behavior during sleep, wake, device changes, and panel closure. Licensing feasibility is established for the listed CC0 candidates subject to provenance; equivalence to Noisli's listening quality is not yet established.

For a ready-made secondary option, [Blankie](https://github.com/codybrom/blankie) is a free native Mac mixer with individual volumes, presets, imports, and a timer. Its source code is MIT, while sounds use separate licenses listed in its [credits](https://blankie.rest/credits/). [Blanket](https://github.com/rafaelmardojai/blanket) provides a Linux precedent and a useful [sound-source index](https://github.com/rafaelmardojai/blanket/blob/master/SOUNDS_LICENSING.md). These demonstrate the approach and offer listening references; neither was installed or tested here.
